# Curriculum

Four parallel tracks. Each session picks work from all four, at the student's current stage in each. Stage advances are earned by criteria, not by calendar.

All pitches are **written alto** unless marked "concert".

---

## Track A: Reading (staff to fingers, no finger charts)

**Starting point:** reads C major scale from staff. Everything else is looked up.

**Mechanism:** note-group ladder. Introduce 2-3 new notes per stage. The generator mixes the new notes with all unlocked notes. Advance when **lookup rate < 10%** over the last 3 exercises with those notes **and** the student plays at the stage tempo.

| Stage | Adds | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | C major, middle octave | consolidate, increase tempo |
| R2 | F#, Bb, Eb | accidentals he already plays, now from the staff. Keys: G, F |
| R3 | Low register stragglers: C#4, D#4/Eb4, and the low C4 | right-pinky table keys and C#. Low Bb3 and B3 are outside his current range |
| R4 | Upper register: C#6, D6, then palm keys D6, Eb6 (D#6 is the current ceiling) | palm keys used in Autumn Leaves; teach palm-key hand shape. E6 and F6 only if the range is later extended |
| R5 | Side Bb, side C, alternate F# | alternate fingerings on purpose, incl. when they help in tunes |
| R6 | Full range with key signatures: all keys in the circle of fifths | the "no chart" circle-of-fifths target |
| R7 | Rhythm reading: eighths, triplets, syncopation, rests, ties, swing notation | separate rhythm-only drills (clap or tap) |
| R8 | Chord-symbol reading and lead sheets | links to the tune library |

Off-horn variants: silent fingering and naming notes, flash-card style, for lip rest days.

## Track B: Theory (from zero, via piano)

Taught with piano as the reference, since he can play and has a strong ear. Each concept is something he can play on the piano first, then on sax.

| Stage | Topic | Done when |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | Half steps, whole steps, the major scale formula (W W H W W W H) | builds any major scale on piano and sax |
| T2 | Key signatures and the circle of fifths | names the sharps/flats of any key, explains why neighbours share notes |
| T3 | Intervals (2nd to octave), major/minor/perfect | identifies intervals by ear and on the staff |
| T4 | Triads and 7th chords: maj7, m7, dom7, m7b5 | spells each on any root, plays arpeggios |
| T5 | Chord function in a key, diatonic 7ths (Imaj7, ii7, V7...) | harmonises a major scale |
| T6 | **ii-V-I** (major and minor) | recognises it in Autumn Leaves, plays it in 3 keys |
| T7 | Modes: Dorian, Mixolydian, as "scales over chords" | explains So What in one sentence |
| T8 | Minor pentatonic and the **blues scale** | plays both in 3 keys, hears the "blue note" |
| T9 | 12-bar blues form and variations | plays the form from memory over a track |
| T10 | Guide tones (3rds and 7ths) and voice leading | plays guide-tone line through Autumn Leaves |

Rule: no theory term is introduced without a 30-second "play it and hear it" example.

## Track C: Jazz and blues language

Keep short, swung, and fun. This track exists because he does not want to sound classical.

| Stage | Content |
|---|---|
| J1 | Swing feel: eighth-note swing, ghost notes, accenting the off-beat. Play a simple scale with a swing feel before anything else |
| J2 | Articulation: "tongue 2, slur 2" patterns on scales and arpeggios |
| J3 | Blues scale licks: 3-5 short, famous-sounding phrases, in 2 keys, learned by ear |
| J4 | Call and response: the tutor/app plays a 2-bar phrase, he answers with a variation |
| J5 | Chord-tone soloing: play only 3rds and 7ths over Autumn Leaves |
| J6 | Motif development: one 2-bar idea, repeat, vary rhythm, vary pitch |
| J7 | Approach notes and enclosures (chromatic and diatonic) |
| J8 | Short transcription: learn a 4-8 bar phrase from a recording **by ear first**, then write it on the staff, then transpose to one other key |
| J9 | Bebop scale and simple bebop vocabulary |
| J10 | Own voice: solos with deliberate choices (space, tone colour, phrasing). Compare to Getz, Rollins, Coltrane phrases |

**Ear track (daily, 5 minutes, can be off-horn):** sing then play 3-5 note patterns; match pitch on piano; learn the head of a tune by ear before looking at notation.

## Track D: Repertoire

Tune work always includes: head from sheet music, then from memory, then the **analysis** (key centres, chord functions, why it works), then improvise.

| Order | Tune | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Autumn Leaves** (easy -> full head) | ii-V-I in major and relative minor, chord tones, 12-bar-style loop. Alto key: E minor / G major written. Move off the YouTube finger-chart video; read the chart (`docs/autumn-leaves-easy.pdf`) |
| 2 | **Naima** | Slow ballad, sustained tones, tone and dynamics. Pedal-point harmony. Great for long-tone control and breath |
| 3 | **Smooth Operator** (Sade) | Groove tune in minor, written D minor (concert F minor) in the Online Sax Academy chart, `docs/smooth-operator-sade.pdf`. Syncopation, tied notes, minor pentatonic/blues colour. The chart has no chord symbols, so chords get added in the tune library |
| 4 | **So What** | D Dorian/Eb Dorian, two modes a half-step apart, simplest route to improvising |
| 5 | **In a Sentimental Mood** | Slow ballad, chord colours, minor to major shift |
| 6 | A basic 12-bar blues (F or Bb concert) | Blues scale, call and response |
| 7 | Tunes from his head | A "tune-from-ear" project each month: pick a melody he hears, find it, write it down |

**Milestone definition for each tune:**
1. Head from the staff, no chart, at slow tempo
2. Head from memory at tempo
3. Chord changes spelled and chord tones played in order
4. 1 chorus improvised over a backing track using chord tones
5. 2+ choruses with motifs and one deliberate blues/pentatonic colour

## Embouchure and tone track (runs through every session)

- **Long tones:** low-intensity, quiet, jaw dropped, tongue high ("ee"), air from the diaphragm. Start with 2-3 min and build.
- **Overtones / voicing:** introduced once the lip can sustain a 3-minute warm-up without biting. Played quietly, in the lower register.
- **Anti-bite protocol:** whenever he reports thin high notes, jaw soreness, or squeaks: stop climbing, check air support, tongue position, jaw drop. If symptoms persist, end the playing block.
- **Endurance ramp (returning after a 4-month break):**
  - Week 1-2: 15-20 min playing blocks, 5-10 min rest, max 2 blocks, minimal high register.
  - Week 3-4: 20-25 min blocks, 3 blocks total with rests.
  - Then add 5 min per block every 2 weeks only if fatigue flags are absent.
  - Rest and reading/theory work off-horn count as practice. The lip does not need to be playing to learn.
- **Reed:** size 2 is soft, which makes biting and thin tone easier. Consider trying a 2.5 reed in 2-3 months, or a better mouthpiece, only when tone is reliable. Not yet.

## Order of high-register work
Hard high-register work (palm keys, altissimo-adjacent) goes **after warm-up, early in the session**, never at the end when the lip is tired.

## Banned until tone is solid
Vibrato, embellishments, growls, bends. (Blue notes are taught as scale degrees first, not bends.)
